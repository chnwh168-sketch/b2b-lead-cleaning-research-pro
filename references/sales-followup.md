# Sales Follow-up Guide

Use this reference when the user asks how to reply, what to send, how to follow up, or how to write an email/WhatsApp message.

## Principle

Research is only useful if it improves the next sales move. Write messages that advance the deal by one concrete step. Avoid generic greetings, long company introductions, and repetitive "please check" follow-ups.

## Choose the objective from the stage

### Fresh inquiry
Reply quickly and answer the actual question first. Then ask only the minimum missing questions needed to quote or recommend correctly.

### Catalog sent
Do not ask "Did you see my catalog?" without value. Ask which model/use case fits and collect quantity/specifications. When appropriate, recommend 1-2 models instead of sending another large catalog.

### Quote sent
Focus on confirming configuration, quantity, destination/incoterm, lead time, and the decision obstacle. Do not restart discovery from zero.

### PI sent
Ask the customer to verify model, quantity, specifications, price, trade terms, consignee/company details, and payment details. The goal is order confirmation/payment, not another generic quotation discussion.

### Existing active customer / spare-parts list
If the list has been approved and preparation is about to begin, explain change-control politely: once preparation starts, items cannot be removed because purchasing/packing will be arranged, but additional items can still be added if operationally true.

## Channel style

### WhatsApp / platform chat
- Short and conversational.
- Usually 1-4 sentences.
- One clear call to action.
- Use the customer's language when useful and reliable.
- Avoid over-formal paragraphs.

### Email
- Give a specific subject.
- Mention the attachment by name/purpose.
- Summarize the key commercial point in the body.
- Ask for the next decision or missing information.
- Keep it concise enough to read on a phone.
- Use the platform's native writing format if available.

## Personalize from research

Use verified context selectively:
- contractor -> reference project/application
- rental company -> durability, maintenance, spare parts, utilization
- dealer/importer -> margin, product-line gap, OEM, after-sales support
- large brand subsidiary -> avoid naive direct replacement pitches; consider OEM, special models, accessories, line-completion, alternative price band
- current project buyer -> focus on spec, delivery, destination, after-sales, and deadline

Do not reveal creepy or unnecessary research details to the customer. Use research to improve relevance, not to show surveillance.

## Useful message patterns

### Send catalog
State that the catalog is attached/sent, then ask quantity + the 1-2 most important specification questions. Offer to recommend the model and quote after confirmation.

### Send PI
State that the PI is attached and reflects the confirmed models/quantities. Ask the customer to check model, specification, quantity, commercial terms, and company/consignee details, then confirm so the order can proceed.

### Follow up after no reply
Add a reason to respond: a recommended model, a technical clarification, freight option, lead-time update, or a simple binary question. Do not send only "Any update?"

### Early-stage Facebook lead
If the customer says "just researching", do not dump a full catalog. Ask what machine/use case they are evaluating, project size/work area, location, and expected purchase month. Then recommend 1-2 suitable models with video and price range.

## Language

When the customer writes in Spanish, Spanish is usually the best default for customer-facing copy unless the user prefers English. Keep Spanish natural and commercial, not literal machine translation.

For internal coaching, explain the sales logic in Chinese unless the user asks otherwise.
